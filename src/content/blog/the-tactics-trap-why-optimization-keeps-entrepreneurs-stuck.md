I almost fell into the tactics trap again.

I had gotten up early and happened to see a video from another marketer. Good guy. Smart guy. He was explaining ways to optimize a webinar or video sales letter.

This is exactly the kind of thing I love.

So naturally, within about three minutes, part of my brain was saying:

**Oh man. He knows something I don't know.**

Maybe this is the thing.

Maybe there is some optimization I have completely missed.

I watched the whole thing, sat back for a minute, and let the excitement wear off.

Come to find out, I had seen the same trap again.

## A tactic is not a system

There is nothing wrong with better tactics.

Better headlines matter. Better follow-up matters. Better landing pages matter. Better offers matter.

But there is a huge difference between improving a working system and believing you cannot start until every tactic is optimized.

That second one will eat you alive.

Tactics are endless.

As soon as you learn one, there are twelve more waiting for you.

The new webinar structure.

The new ad format.

The new AI tool.

The new email framework.

The new script.

The new way to build the landing page.

If your standard for launching is **"I understand all of it now,"** you may never launch.

## Ask a much better question

When I catch myself doing this, the question I want to come back to is:

**What are the absolute minimum things I need in order to run this campaign?**

Not the perfect version.

The version that works well enough to get into motion.

If you want to host an online class, you probably do not need the world's greatest webinar funnel before you invite anyone.

You need a topic.

You need people to invite.

You need a way for them to show up.

And you need something useful to teach.

You can improve everything else after the first one.

If you want to make a video, your first requirement may be a phone and something worth saying.

If you want to build referral relationships, you may not need a 17-step referral-partner nurture sequence.

You may need to call ten people and invite a couple of them to lunch.

## Experience teaches you what to optimize

This is the part I think gets backwards.

We often try to learn enough optimization **before** doing the work.

But once you actually run the campaign, the campaign starts teaching you.

People do not respond to the invitation? Good. Now you have something specific to improve.

People register but do not show up? There is another problem you can work on.

The video gets watched but nobody takes the next step? Now we can look at the offer.

Those are useful optimization problems because they are attached to reality.

That is completely different from sitting at your desk worrying about every possible thing that could be wrong.

## Start the campaign before you master the campaign

Entrepreneurs have almost unlimited access to information now.

That is a gigantic advantage.

It can also become a gigantic hiding place.

There will always be another tactic.

Another expert.

Another optimization.

Another piece you could add.

So if there is a campaign you have wanted to run, simplify the question.

**What is the minimum viable version I can actually put into motion?**

Build that one.

Run it.

Then let the real world tell you what deserves to be improved.
