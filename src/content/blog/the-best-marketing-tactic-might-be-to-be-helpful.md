We talk a lot about tactics.

Hooks.

Ads.

Emails.

Videos.

Follow-up.

Algorithms.

But one of the best marketing tactics I know is almost embarrassingly simple:

**Be helpful.**

Be as helpful as you can to as many people in your world as you reasonably can.

That is what "providing value" is supposed to mean before we turned it into marketing language.

## You do not have to know everything

This is one of the things that stops people from creating content.

They think they have to sound like the world's leading authority before they are allowed to say anything.

You don't.

You don't have to know everything.

You don't have to sound perfect.

You don't need the perfect studio.

You don't have to turn every thought into a masterclass.

You just have to know something that could help the person on the other side.

That might be one thing you learned this morning.

A mistake you made.

A simple explanation.

A shortcut.

A recommendation.

A question somebody asked you three times this week.

That is enough to start.

## You have a way of helping that belongs to you

Two people can understand the exact same subject and explain it completely differently.

That is a good thing.

Your experience changes the examples you use.

Your personality changes the way you explain it.

The mistakes you have made change what you warn people about.

The people you work with change what you notice.

You do not have to manufacture a "personal brand" before you can create useful content.

A lot of your brand starts showing up naturally when you consistently help people in your own way.

## Helpful is easier than impressive

This shift also does something interesting to fear.

If the goal of your video is:

**I need everybody to think I am impressive,**

you have created a pretty brutal assignment.

Now the lighting matters more.

Your words matter more.

Every stumble feels bigger.

But if the assignment is:

**There is somebody who could use this. Let me explain it to them,**

the whole thing changes.

You stop thinking quite so much about yourself.

You start thinking about them.

That tends to make the real version of you show up.

## It works outside content too

This is not only a content idea.

Pick up the phone because you might be able to help somebody.

Send the email because you found something useful.

Reach out to a past client because there is something they should know.

Invite somebody to an event because it could genuinely benefit them.

Marketing gets a lot less weird when the first question is not:

**How do I get a lead out of this?**

It is:

**How can I be useful here?**

The leads, referrals, reputation, and relationships can grow out of that.

But helpful comes first.
