# Content Standards

## What Good Renegades Content Feels Like
A business owner should finish a piece thinking one or more of:
- "That makes sense."
- "I can actually do that."
- "I have something like this already."
- "This is simpler than I thought."
- "I should put a system around this."
- "I want to try that."

## Voice
Direct. Conversational. Useful. Optimistic without pretending business is easy.

Darin can be personal, funny, opinionated about marketing practices, and candid about mistakes. Do not manufacture bravado or turn every idea into a manifesto.

## Structure
Strong default:
1. recognizable problem/opportunity
2. useful explanation
3. concrete examples
4. simple framework or next action
5. natural conclusion/CTA

Do not force listicles or numbered frameworks when normal prose works better.

## Titles
Prefer clear curiosity over clickbait.

Good territory:
- "Marketing That Actually Gets Done"
- "Your Database Is Probably Your Best Untapped Marketing Asset"
- "One Piece of Content Can Become a Marketing System"

Avoid:
- fake urgency
- impossible promises
- "secret hack" language
- generic AI titles
- excessive punctuation/capitalization

## Claims
If a source contains a specific statistic or business-performance claim, verify it when practical before publishing.

Do not repeat weak internet statistics just because they sound useful.

When a claim is based on Darin's personal experience, frame it as experience rather than universal proof.

## Brand Connection
Content does not have to pitch a product.

The content should naturally make the Renegades worldview visible:
- organize marketing
- use systems
- automate repeatable work
- stay human where humans matter
- work the relationships/assets already available
- create consistency
- use modern leverage without making the business more complicated

## Calls to Action
Match the CTA to the reader's next logical step.

Examples:
- learn more in another article
- listen to the related podcast
- join the free Community
- see Renegade CRM
- explore Renegade Academy
- attend a Power Hour

Avoid stuffing every offer into every piece.

## Categories
Use a small stable set. Current preferred categories:
- Marketing Systems
- Database Marketing
- Lead Generation
- Lead Follow-Up
- Reviews & Referrals
- Content & Visibility
- Referral Partners
- Events
- AI & Marketing Tech
- Mindset & Business

Add a category only when it represents a real recurring editorial lane.

## Podcast Standards
Show: **No Fear Marketing Show**

The podcast can include:
- solo teaching
- interviews
- Power Hours adapted to audio
- useful conversations
- experiments/case studies
- marketing-tech walkthroughs that still work without visuals

If an episode depends heavily on seeing a screen, either create a useful audio edit or make it a video/article instead.

## Historical Content
Use genuine original dates for migrations.

Do not backdate newly created articles to make the site look older.

It is fine to revive an older idea today and say it was adapted from an earlier talk, email, video, or episode.

## Repurposing
Repurposing should increase leverage, not create sameness.

A transcript is source material, not automatically a finished article.

Adapt each format:
- podcast = listenable conversation/teaching
- article = readable argument
- email = personal direct note
- short = one idea/hook
- playbook = implementation steps
