# Renegade Writing Style

Every piece in `queue/` and `src/content/blog/` is written to this. Read it before writing anything.

## The target

A smart magazine column by an experienced entrepreneur. It shouldn't read like a sales letter. Imagine a friend who has run businesses for twenty-five years explaining something over lunch: funny, a little irreverent, specific, and not in a hurry.

- **Flowing paragraphs.** Develop each thought properly. Vary sentence length. A short standalone line is fine now and then, as seasoning, but it shouldn't be the structure of the piece.
- **Humor that comes from observation:** funny metaphors, cultural references, self-deprecation, conversational asides, the absurd little truths of running a business.
- **Darin's voice patterns:** he puts himself physically into the scene, uses parenthetical asides, tells stories in present tense, pokes fun at himself, and signs off warmly.
- **Broad product, narrow message.** One piece solves one problem. Renegade CRM shows up as the obvious tool for that problem, and never as a feature tour.
- **Useful even if nobody clicks.** The reader should be able to act on the idea without buying anything.

## When Darin supplies a draft

Keep 80–90% of his wording, rhythm, stories, jokes, and point of view. Fix clarity, flow, transitions, and repetition. Don't polish him out of his own piece.

## Banned phrases

In today's fast-paced digital world/landscape · delve into · look no further · whether you're X or Y · at its core · unlock the power of · harness the power of · elevate · navigate the complexities of · foster · underscore · seamless/seamlessly · robust · game-changing · transformative · cutting-edge · pivotal · crucial · tapestry · a testament to · it's not just X, it's Y · in conclusion · the bottom line is · here's the thing most people miss · and that changes everything · the truth is far less obvious · what if everything you've been told about X is wrong · the secret experts don't want you to know · but here's what nobody tells you · read that again · here's the dirty little secret · let that sink in

## Banned rhythms

- Fake revelations and manufactured contrarianism
- Leaning on "X isn't the problem, Y is" or "You don't need X, you need Y"
- Stacks of dramatic one-line paragraphs
- Fake mic-drop endings
- Strings of rhetorical questions
- Generic LinkedIn-influencer cadence

## Channel notes

- **Blog:** 700–1,100 words. Two or three subheads at most, and only where the piece actually changes direction. End on the practical next step and the CTA, with no grand summary.
- **Email:** 150–300 words, one idea, one link. It should read like Darin wrote to one person.
- **Facebook / LinkedIn:** 120–250 words. It has to deliver value in the feed. Put the link at the end, after the point has already landed.
- **Instagram:** 80–150 words. No links. Use "link in bio" or a comment keyword.

## Claims

Present Darin's experience as experience, never as universal proof. Don't invent statistics, customers, or results. If a number matters, cite it or cut it.
